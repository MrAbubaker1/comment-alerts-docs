Getting an email when someone comments on a Jira issue

Three ways to do it in Jira Cloud, and the honest limits of each.

Documentation Notify only the assignee Automation rules

Jira Cloud already emails people when a comment is added. Most of the time the complaint is not that nothing arrives — it is that the wrong people get it, or that the message is unreadable, or that it stopped arriving and nobody knows why. Each of those has a different fix.

Start by working out which one you actually have.

1. The notification scheme — free, and usually the right answer

Every Jira project has a notification scheme: a list of events, and for each event, who gets told. Issue Commented is one of those events.

  1. Open your project settings

    Project settings → Notifications. You will see the scheme the project uses and the recipients for each event.

  2. Find the Issue Commented row

    It lists who is notified — typically Current Assignee, Reporter, All Watchers, and sometimes a project role or a whole group.

  3. Edit the scheme

    You need Jira administrator rights, and the scheme is usually shared with other projects. Changing it changes those projects too, so make a copy first if you are not sure.

This handles the majority of cases and costs nothing. If your problem is too many people are being emailed, this is where you fix it — see notifying only the assignee and reporter.

Why it might not be enough

  • You cannot change the wording. Jira Cloud does not let administrators edit notification email templates. What Atlassian sends is what your team reads, including everyone outside your team who gets copied in.
  • Jira does not email you about your own actions by default. Each person controls that under their own personal settings, so “I never get notified” is often a personal setting rather than a project one.
  • Recipients are Jira roles, not addresses. If you want a message to reach a shared inbox, a distribution list, or somebody without a Jira licence, the scheme has nothing to offer you.
  • There is no record. When somebody says they never got the email, there is nothing to look at.

2. An automation rule — free within limits, and flexible

Jira Automation can watch for a comment and send an email you write yourself. Project settings → Automation → Create rule, trigger Issue commented, action Send email.

This is the right tool when you want unusual routing: notify a specific person only when a keyword appears, only on one issue type, only when the priority is high. It is genuinely powerful and it is included in your Jira plan.

Its limits are worth knowing before you build on it. Automation rules consume a monthly execution allowance that depends on your Jira plan, and a busy project can spend it quickly. Formatting the comment body inside an automation email is fiddly. And a rule is a piece of configuration somebody has to maintain — rules quietly break, and when they do, nothing tells you.

There is a fuller walkthrough in sending comment emails with an automation rule, including what it looks like when it goes wrong.

3. A Marketplace app

An app makes sense when you want the message itself to be yours: your wording, your subject line, your sender address, going out through your own email account rather than Atlassian’s — and a log showing what was sent and to whom.

Comment Alerts for Jira does exactly that and nothing else. On a new comment it emails the issue’s assignee and reporter — those two people, no one else, and both must be Jira users with an address the app can read — using a subject and body you write, sent through your own Resend account. Every attempt is recorded on the settings page with its result, so a message that never arrived shows up with a reason instead of vanishing.

Be aware of what it asks of you in return: you need a Resend account and a verified sending domain before it will deliver to anyone. That is a DNS change and ten minutes of setup. If nobody at your company can add a DNS record, one of the two free options above will serve you better.

Which one to pick

If your problem is…Use
Too many people are notifiedThe notification scheme
Nobody is notifiedThe notification scheme, then personal settings
Only notify in certain conditionsAn automation rule
The email needs to say something differentAn app
Mail must reach a shared inbox or a non-Jira addressAn automation rule
You need proof of what was sentAn app

Two of those three cost nothing. If one of them solves your problem, use it.
